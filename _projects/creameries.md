**Status:** Active — co-authored

The late 19th century saw a wave of cooperative creamery formation across Denmark and Ireland. This cluster of papers uses firm-level data and causal designs to ask why some creameries succeeded and others didn't.

One paper examines how **religious divisions** shaped cooperative formation and productivity — testing whether social capital and trust operate through denominational boundaries. A second paper exploits the **geography of peat bogs** as a natural experiment in energy access: proximity to peat (the dominant fuel) is a geographical lottery that allows clean identification of energy shocks on industrial productivity.

Together, the papers show how institutions, geography, and energy jointly determined the trajectory of early agricultural industrialization.
